If you want to become the most popular person at the local pond — or the favorite “parent” to your backyard flock — keep one thing in your pocket: peas.

While many people still toss soggy white bread (essentially junk food for waterfowl), savvy duck keepers know that peas are a true superfood that keeps ducks healthy, hydrated, and entertained.

### 1. The Nutritional Secret Weapon

Unlike bread, which offers almost no nutrition and can contribute to Angel Wing, peas deliver real value:

- **Protein** for feather and muscle maintenance
- **Niacin (Vitamin B3)** — critical for leg strength and bone development (a deficiency can cause bowed legs, especially in heavy breeds)
- **Vitamins A & E** for immune support and healthy, waterproof feathers
- **Fiber** to support digestion

### 2. Why Peas Beat Bread at the Park

Bread creates several problems: it rots in the water, fuels algae blooms, and attracts pests. Peas are a much better alternative because:

- They float long enough for ducks to dabble naturally
- Uneaten peas break down safely or get eaten by fish
- They are nutrient-dense, so ducks fill up on something useful instead of empty carbs

### 3. The “Pea Bob”: Mental Enrichment

Dropping a handful of peas into a shallow dish or kiddie pool creates an instant foraging game. Ducks will spend a long time snorkeling and diving for every last one. This mimics natural behavior and helps reduce boredom, flock drama, and feather picking.

### 4. The Golden Rule: Fresh or Frozen — Never Canned

**Fresh peas** from the garden or produce section are excellent.

**Frozen peas** are especially useful in hot weather. Tossing them into the water acts like nutritious ice cubes that help cool the birds while they snack. (Thaw or crush them for very young ducklings.)

**Canned peas should never be fed to ducks.**  
They are packed with sodium. Ducks have sensitive kidneys and cannot process high levels of salt. Canned peas can cause salt poisoning, dehydration, tremors, and even death.

### Final Verdict

Leave the bread in the pantry. Grab a bag of fresh or frozen peas instead. It’s better for the water, better for their legs and overall health, and — judging by the enthusiastic response you’ll get — much better for the ducks.
