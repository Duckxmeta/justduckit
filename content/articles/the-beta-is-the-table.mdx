The boxes sold out. The cards left Chelsea. eBay filled up with singles and playsets. That part of the story is already written.

What is still open is the door.

Go to ddltcg.com/play right now. No box required. No seed phrase. No waitlist gate. The digital beta for Doginal Dogs Legends is live. Same 40 HP. Same five lanes. Same 40-card decks built from the same classes — Wizard, Crown, Zombie, Bow, Pirate. You can learn the game tonight against a bot, then queue a PvP match before the weekend is over.

That is the move the Pack is making while the secondary market prices the physical set.

## Physical proof, digital practice

Rise of the Pack did what most crypto products never do. It left the timeline and became an object people bid on, ship, and sleeve. Three weeks after DDNYC the search term still returns hundreds of listings. Chase cards move. Playsets get assembled. Master sets show up with custom slabs and event merch attached.

That is the receipt for the physical product.

The digital beta is the receipt for the design. The same rules that ran a live tournament under Dream Downtown lights now run in a browser. You do not have to own a first-edition box to understand why the lanes matter or why a trap sitting quiet can change the swing. You open the site, hit Teach, and the game teaches you.

Most projects treat digital as a second merch channel or a future promise. Legends treated it as the practice table. Physical is the collectible and the competitive object. Digital is the open floor where anyone can learn the language before they sit across from a person who already owns the cardboard.

## What the beta actually gives you

You start at 40 HP. Your opponent starts at 40 HP. You build a 40-card deck with a max of three copies of any card. Mana climbs to 10. Five lanes. Creatures hold ground and attack when ready. Spells resolve now. Traps wait. Drop the other Hero to zero and you win.

That is the whole spine. No novel of keywords. No banned-list archaeology. The digital client mirrors the physical game so the transfer is clean. What you learn against the bot is what you will do at a kitchen table or in a future bracket.

Modes are already there: tutorial, bots, PvP, daily tournaments. Miles track time played. Leaderboards exist. People in the Pack are posting links to deck builders and brackets the same way they post floor screenshots. The infrastructure of a real game is showing up in public, not behind a paid pass.

## Why free-to-play is the point

The origin of the Dogs was free. Ten thousand inscriptions went to the community. No founder allocation. No mint revenue. That choice still shapes the culture.

Legends continues the same pattern in a different medium. The first-edition physical set is limited and already trading at a premium. The digital beta is not. Anyone with a browser can sit down and play. That removes the biggest barrier for new players and for holders who never bought a box. You do not have to justify a purchase to learn whether you like the game. You just play.

When the next set arrives, or when more physical product finds its way into more hands, the people who already know the lanes will be ready. The ones who only watched the tournament clips will have already swung a few turns. The onboarding pipeline is open instead of gated.

## The rooms keep multiplying

While the beta runs, the Pack is still moving in the real world. Bark Media Africa is building the African arm of the community — onboarding, Spaces, local energy. Miami dates are on the calendar for December. Daily CSN broadcasts continue. The same people who ripped packs in Chelsea are now posting about the next city and the next table.

None of that is separate from the game. The digital client is another room. The secondary market is another room. The Africa takeover is another room. The consistency is the same: show up, build the thing, leave the door open.

A hand-drawn TCG that sells out and then opens a free digital practice table is rare. A free-mint inscription collection that turns into that TCG is rarer. The Pack keeps choosing the version of the story that compounds instead of the version that extracts.

## What to do with the open door

If you have never played, open ddltcg.com/play and run the tutorial. One match against a bot is enough to feel the rhythm. If you already own product, use the digital side to test decks and teach the people around you. If you are waiting on physical, the beta keeps the skill growing while the cards circulate.

The boxes are gone from the shop. The cards are not gone from the culture. The beta is the table that stays open after the event lights go down.

Shuffle the digital deck. Swing a lane. Then bring what you learned to the next real table.

The Pack does not wait for perfect inventory. It builds the practice floor and keeps the door unlocked.
