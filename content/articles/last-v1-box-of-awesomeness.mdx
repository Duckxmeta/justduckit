One of the last unopened V1 Boxes of Awesomeness just left the eBay list.

Emeta777 posted the delisting on November 15. Official Dogs account shared it the same day. TruckMeta wrote it up on the 16th: pack is noticing the physical pieces getting scarce.

That is the move in the last 24 hours. Not another floor screenshot. Not another Spaces promo. A holder pulling the remaining sealed box off the public market.

We have watched the on-chain dogs trade for years. We have watched the Legends cards leave the New York hotel and start living on kitchen tables and secondary listings. The V1 boxes are older. They were the first physical objects the pack could hold that were not a screenshot of an inscription. Limited runs. Event-tied. Made for the people who showed up in rooms or ordered from the shop before the next drop replaced them.

When one of the last sealed ones gets pulled, the scarcity is no longer theoretical.

## What the box actually was

Box of Awesomeness is not a slogan. It is the product name the shop used for the premium merch packages that shipped with never-before-released collectibles, gear, and the kind of pieces that only existed because someone decided to print them, pack them, and hand them out or sell them once.

V1 means the first version. The one that predates the Legends TCG boxes, the Miami packages, the later runs. Those later ones still exist. Official shop still lists a DDMiami Box of Awesomeness at times, pickup-only, sold out or low stock depending on the day. The V1s are the ones that already left the building years ago.

Holders who kept them sealed treated them like the dogs themselves: something you do not casually list and then re-list. When the last few start disappearing from public marketplaces, the rest of the pack sees it. The official account amplifies it. The radio write-up notes it. That is how a community marks the moment an object stops being available and starts being history.

## Scarcity that is earned, not printed

Most NFT merch is infinite. Print more. Restock. Drop again next month. The physical side of Doginal Dogs has run differently. Event boxes, limited gear, the pieces that only moved because people showed up in New York or Miami or Toronto and then carried the object home.

The free mint on Dogecoin gave the inscriptions away. The physical product did not. It required decisions about what to make, how many, and who gets to hold the sealed version. Once those decisions are made and the boxes leave the warehouse or the hotel table, the supply is fixed. Delisting the last unopened one is not a price pump. It is a holder deciding the object is worth more in the vault than on the open market.

We have seen the same pattern with the Rise of the Pack cards. Preorder sold out. Debut at DDNYC. Then secondary listings thickened because people who opened packs wanted the chase pieces in other hands. The V1 boxes are further along that curve. There is no new print run waiting. The sealed ones that remain are the ones someone chose not to open and not to sell until now.

That is the free-mint-to-IRL pipeline working in reverse. The culture started digital and free. The objects that survived became scarce because the people who received them treated them as permanent.

## The rooms that made the boxes

The boxes existed because the rooms existed. DDNYC 2026 put packs in hands under the lights at Dream Downtown. Earlier events put earlier boxes in hands. The shop runs the made-to-order and pickup-only drops so the next city does not require inventing inventory from nothing.

When a pack mate pulls the last V1 off eBay, the signal is not that the brand is finished. It is that the earlier chapters already closed. The next room is already on the calendar. DD Miami sits in early December. Championship dates are set. Graded Legends cards are already circulating. The same holders who kept the V1 sealed are the ones who will show up with decks and duffles in Wynwood.

Physical product from on-chain IP only compounds if the community keeps both layers alive. The inscription stays on Dogecoin forever. The sealed box only stays sealed because someone decided the object itself was worth preserving. Both decisions are the same culture.

## What we do with the signal

If you hold an unopened V1, the delisting is the receipt that other holders are treating the remaining supply as finite. If you are hunting one, the public lists just got thinner. If you are building toward Miami, the same pack that notices when the last box leaves the market is the pack that fills the next room.

We do not need to invent sold prices or claim every sealed box is now gone. The post exists. The official share exists. The radio note exists. The direction is enough. Early physical product is aging into collectible status the same way the cards did after they left the event.

The dogs started as inscriptions anyone could mint. The boxes started as objects only some people could hold. Both are still here. One of the last sealed V1s just stopped being for sale.

Proof the pull. Keep the vault closed or open it on purpose. Then say see you in Miami.
