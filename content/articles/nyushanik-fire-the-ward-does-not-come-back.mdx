On the night of 7 October a Russian strike hit Sadiba Nyushanik, a bird rehabilitation center in Obukhiv district, Kyiv Oblast. The inpatient ward burned. So did the henhouse and the quail house. Founder Iryna Snopko said the birds and a cat were inside, and so was the equipment and the feed. By morning the count from her was about 190 birds dead. The ward, she said, cannot be repaired.

This is not a metaphor for how hard animal care is. It is a building that held the clinic, and the clinic is gone.

## What burned, named

Snopko posted the loss list, and Ukrainska Pravda carried it in an update the same evening. Five wild birds died in the fire: a blind hobby, a honey buzzard, a sparrowhawk, a red-footed falcon, and a boreal owl on Ukraine's Red Book. With them, about 150 quail, 30 hens, and two roosters. A three-legged cat named Papushok is missing.

An English write-up on EcoPolitic the next morning swapped several of those names — lesser spotted eagle, red-backed shrike, long-eared owl. The founder's list is the one that counts. If you are sending money or repeating the story, use her birds, not the translation.

The gear list is the part a sanctuary operator reads twice. Generator. Inverter. Solar panels. Two refrigerators and a freezer. Oxygen concentrator. Air conditioner. Humidifier. Nebulizer. Germicidal lamp. Surgical tools and rehab gear. Almost a tonne of compound feed, frozen insects, live roaches, mealworms, and a stock of rodents for the raptors. Medicines. A collection of rare feathers and bird literature, gone with the rest. Snopko's line, via Ukrainska Pravda: they could not count everything lost in two hours. The main loss was the birds.

Ukrinform put the center in Obukhiv district and noted the NGO has been rehabilitating raptors and waterfowl for more than 15 years — storks, swans, owls, hawks, kestrels, the birds that cannot make it on their own. The farther aviaries did not catch. EcoPolitic, citing Suspilne and Snopko, said herons, owls, gulls, harriers, crows, and other wild birds are still there. Peregrine falcons were named among the survivors who now have no pharmacy and no feed room.

Clearing started at 7 a.m. The ward has to be built again. Not patched.

## The quiet inventory is the real bill

Anyone who has run a pen knows the fire is the loud part. The next week is the bill you cannot see from a video.

A raptor ward is a cold chain and an oxygen line as much as it is a cage. Frozen insects and a freezer full of rodents are not a pantry you restock at the co-op on Saturday. They are the diet. An oxygen concentrator and a nebulizer are how a smoke-injured lung gets a chance. Solar and a generator are how that gear runs when the grid is already a rumor. Lose the panels, the inverter, and the generator in the same night and you are not "without power." You are without the only reason the remaining birds can be treated where they stand.

Feed math does not care that the story is a war story. In 2024 this same crew raised 17 orphaned white storks and turned them loose. Snopko told Ukrainska Pravda the chicks ate about 15 kilograms a day, and the monthly buy for that one group ran near a tonne. That was a good year, with a building. The birds still in the far pens this morning do not have that tonne. They have whatever was not in the room that burned.

We keep a sanctuary in rural Tennessee. Different war, same physics. A clinic is a pile of unglamorous objects: the freezer, the oxygen, the lamp, the labeled bottles, the feed you bought before you needed it. When those objects go, the animals left alive become an emergency with a clock. You can love birds all day. If the nebulizer is slag, love is not a treatment.

## What this place actually did

Nyushanik is not a petting barn that got unlucky. The public record is releases.

The 17 storks in 2024 were raised from small, fed raw meat first, then a wider diet, kept in an open-air aviary, and taught to hunt on the pattern of wild storks nesting nearby. One of them was Katrusia, the smallest chick from a filmed pair, thrown from the nest by the father. She was treated, paired for a while with a chick pulled off a nest in Chernihiv Oblast, and later left with a flock of her own.

This year the same center took a black vulture that had hatched in Germany, come through Bulgaria, and arrived in Cherkasy Oblast exhausted and wounded. Antibiotics for three weeks. Bandage changes after that, because the wound was slow. Snopko said the bird bloodied her husband more than once when he got his strength back. They released him.

That is the work that lived in the ward. The ward is the thing that does not come back on its own.

A sanctuary that just lost its clinic does not need a thread about resilience. It needs feed, a freezer, oxygen, and a room that can be locked and cleaned. The birds in the far pens are the whole remaining program. Everything else is a rebuild.

Count the freezer. Then count the birds.
