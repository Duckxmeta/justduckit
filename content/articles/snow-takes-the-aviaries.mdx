Wet snow does not care about the design notes. At World Bird Sanctuary in Valley Park, Missouri, the Thanksgiving storm dropped enough heavy, sticky snow that the netting on the aviary tops stopped letting it through. The weight pulled posts, snapped cables, and tore mesh. Twelve large enclosures in the public viewing area went down. Half of them will likely need to be rebuilt from scratch. The place is closed until further notice.

Executive director Roger Holloway has been there 38 years. He called it the worst weather damage he has seen. Staff and trainers were out on Thanksgiving moving birds. None of the resident birds in those aviaries were hurt in the collapse itself. Some flew into nearby trees. By early afternoon on the 26th only three were still loose. Later reporting from St. Louis Public Radio said 13 aviaries were critically damaged, 21 birds had to be evacuated, and as of Friday afternoon two were still unaccounted for—Gordon the Barred Owl and Mr. Peabody the Tawny Owl. The sanctuary said it was not asking the public to help locate them. The raptor hospital that treats wild birds stayed open.

## The Numbers That Hit a Sanctuary Budget

Holloway told reporters some of the newer enclosures cost around $30,000 each. Multiply that across a dozen and the repair bill is not a weekend project. Insurance is expected to cover part of it. The rest is donations. The official recovery page is the Qgiv link the sanctuary posted: secure.qgiv.com/for/worldbirdsanctuary/. They are taking it in 30-day chunks—clear the sites, rebuild, then put birds back. No reopening date yet. Next year is their 50th anniversary. They had planned to mark it without having to dig out from a calamity.

The aviaries were designed so snow and leaves would pass through the tops. Wet snow sticks. Wind overnight added load. That is the mechanical fact. For anyone who has built or maintained outdoor housing for birds, the failure mode is familiar: the system works until the one weather event that exceeds the assumption. Then the posts come out of the ground or the cables let go, and the birds that live there full-time need a new plan before the next storm.

## Why This Lands on a Builder Who Also Runs a Sanctuary

We keep ducks on a rural Tennessee property. Our enclosures are smaller and our weather is different, but the operational math is the same. You design for the ordinary load. You maintain. You still get the event that was outside the design case. When it hits, the first job is the animals. The second is documenting what failed so the rebuild is stronger, not just a copy of what just came down. The third is money, because even a well-run nonprofit or a small private sanctuary does not carry the cash for twelve enclosures at once.

World Bird Sanctuary is not a duck place. It is a long-running raptor and bird sanctuary with a hospital that sees hundreds of injured birds of prey a year and more than 200 resident animals across dozens of species. The public side is how a lot of people first meet the work. Closing it removes the visitor revenue and the quiet education that happens when someone walks the trails and sees the birds in actual outdoor housing instead of a photo. The hospital stays open, which matters. The permanent residents that lived in the damaged aviaries still need secure space while the structures are rebuilt. That is the part that does not show up in a headline but determines whether the next month is calm or a scramble.

## What Moved in the Last 48 Hours

The storm hit Wednesday night into Thanksgiving. Damage was reported and the sanctuary posted its closure on the 26th. Holloway was on local news the same day describing the scope. By the 27th a congressman-elect had walked the property with staff. All birds from the damaged aviaries had been relocated with no injuries reported in that update. Cleanup volunteers are being recruited. Donations are coming in. The missing-owl status was still open as of the Friday afternoon post cited by STLPR. That is the live timeline as of this morning.

## The Closer

Sanctuaries run on the assumption that the housing holds. When the housing does not, everything else is downstream. The birds come first. Then the posts and cables. Then the calendar that just lost months of normal operations right before a milestone year. If you run outdoor enclosures of any size, this is the reminder that the design load is a number you chose, not a guarantee the weather signed. World Bird Sanctuary is collecting support at the Qgiv link while they work through the 30-day chunks. The hospital is still taking birds. The public side is not. That is the concrete thing that happened.
