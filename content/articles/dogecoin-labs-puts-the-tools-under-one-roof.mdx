The chain the dogs live on just put its wallet, its app layer, and its merchant payments under one roof.

On October 21 Dogecoin Labs launched as the Foundation’s official commercial partner. It will own and run three products that used to sit in different places: the MyDoge wallet, the DogeOS application layer, and Such App for merchant payments. Jordan Jefferson, who founded MyDoge and DogeOS, is CEO. House of Doge stepped back from its exclusive deal for an equity stake and a license. The Foundation keeps the open-source and stewardship role it has always held.

That is not a roadmap slide. That is three existing products moving under one company the same week the pack is still ripping Rise of the Pack cards on kitchen tables and posting the DDMIAMI schedule.

## The three products, as they stand

MyDoge is the consumer wallet. The company says it crossed 500,000 users without paid acquisition and recently rolled out V3, which connects holders to applications being built on DogeOS. It is the front door most people already know.

DogeOS is the application environment. It is EVM-compatible, so builders can use the same tooling they already know from other chains. Its public testnet, Chikyū, opened on September 30. No mainnet date has been given. Featured applications on the public guide are still marked coming soon. The testnet is live. The apps are not yet.

Such App handles merchant payments. It is in beta for invoicing and in-person QR payments. A DogeOS integration is planned. It is the piece that tries to turn “I hold DOGE” into “I can pay for something with DOGE without leaving the room.”

Together they form the obvious stack: build the app on DogeOS, put it in front of holders through MyDoge, and let merchants accept the coin through Such App. That is the pitch. Whether it ships on that schedule is still open.

## Why this matters on a chain that already has culture

Doginal Dogs did not wait for a product company. The inscriptions are already there. The physical cards are already in hands. People are already pulling legendaries, grading full arts, and listing them. The next room — Miami in December — already has a published schedule: Rip and Sip, Dogs at Sea, the championship bracket.

Labs is not the culture. Labs is the commercial side of the base layer the culture sits on. For years the complaint about Dogecoin has been the same: massive holders, thin everyday tools. The Foundation stayed non-commercial. House of Doge and the individual teams moved product. Now those product teams are in one organization with a clear commercial mandate while the Foundation keeps its original job.

The pack has been living the other half of that sentence. Free mint to identity to physical object to table. The dogs are the proof that people will show up and build objects on this chain even when the utility layer is still thin. Labs is the attempt to thicken that layer without turning the whole thing into a single company’s roadmap.

## Concrete rooms, then the lesson

Look at what is already moving this week. Holders are posting unboxing videos of Rise of the Pack. Official accounts are amplifying the rips. Graded cards are appearing on secondary markets. The DDMIAMI schedule went live on the 20th. People are already booking the Arlo Wynwood and talking about the yacht night and the daytime card rips.

None of that required DogeOS to be on mainnet. The culture and the cardboard do not pause while the app layer finishes its testnet. That is the point. The pack has been betting that showing up in rooms and shipping physical product is enough to keep the chain interesting. Labs is now betting that a dedicated product company can give those same holders more places to actually spend or build with the coin.

We do not need to pretend the testnet is production or that 500,000 wallets means 500,000 daily active users. Those numbers are claims from the companies involved. What is not a claim is the structure: one company now owns the wallet, the app environment, and the payments tool, with the Foundation still separate. That is a cleaner map than three disconnected teams.

## What we do with it

If you hold dogs or cards, the job has not changed. Keep the table open. Keep the rips public. Keep the Miami dates on the calendar. The product company does not replace the rooms. It sits underneath them.

If you build, the testnet is open. The tooling is familiar. The distribution path through MyDoge is already larger than most new chains can claim. Whether that turns into real applications people use every day is the part that still has to be proven in public.

The dogs are already inscribed. The cards are already in hands. The next room is already booked. Labs just put the tools that are supposed to make the coin useful under one roof so the people who already show up have more options when they get there.

Proof the product. Drop the next set. Keep walking into the rooms.

See you in Miami.
