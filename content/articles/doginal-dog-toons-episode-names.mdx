Someone in the pack sat down and named seven episodes.

Puddle Jumpers. Treasure Raft. Funfair at Dusk. Cardboard Rocket Ride. Deep Blue Peeble. The Lost Ball. Follow the Falling Star.

Duvalx posted the list on Halloween night with a short clip. By morning the official @doginaldogs account had boosted it. The pack started picking favorites in the replies. No press release. No roadmap slide. Just titles, a video, and the dogs already in the frame.

That is the part that matters.

## Creators do not wait for permission

Most collections treat community content like free advertising. The Dogs treat it like the actual product layer.

We have watched the same pattern for two years. Official ships the dogs, the cards, the events, the plush. Then holders and adjacent creators pick up the IP and run. Radio shows. Spaces. Graded cards. Community tournaments. Now cartoons.

Duvalx is not new to this. The bio says hotels, crypto, NFTs, skate videos. The post is the same energy that fills rooms at DDNYC and Miami: people who already know how to make things and decided the dogs belong in the next thing they make.

The titles are specific. Puddle Jumpers is not "Episode 1." Treasure Raft is not a placeholder. Someone spent time on the names the way the team spent time on the hand-drawn Legends cards. That is not generative. That is a person deciding what the dogs do next.

The toons post is the clearest signal that the pack has moved past waiting for the next official drop to have something to talk about.

## Handmade still beats the feed

We have seen what the rest of crypto does when volume is down. AI avatars. Generic pixel drops. Infinite generative sets that nobody can name.

Doginal Dogs started the other way. Ten thousand dogs, each one looked at. Legends cards drawn by hand. Events that require a plane ticket. Gigatron and the agent swarm are the newest layer, and even those still have humans signing off.

Toons sit in the same lane. Episode names that sound like Saturday morning. A clip that puts the dogs in motion. The official account did not commission it. It amplified it. That is how a brand stays alive after the mint.

The alternative is the feed full of projects that only exist when the team posts. The pack does not run that way. Holders organize Charlotte tournaments. ## Rooms, then screens, then rooms again

DDNYC proved the rooms work. Rise of the Pack proved the cards leave the hotel and hit kitchen tables. The toons prove the same loop can run in reverse: someone makes the thing, the pack sees it, the official layer blesses it, and now there are seven episodes people can ask about in the next Space.

We do not know the production schedule. We do not know if the full episodes exist yet or if the titles are the start. The post asked which one is the favorite. That is enough to keep the conversation going until the next clip drops.

What we do know is the pattern. The dogs were free. The community filled the rooms. The cards sold out. Miami is already sold out. The agents are running tasks in Gigatron. And somewhere a creator decided the next logical step was to put the dogs in a cardboard rocket.

That is not a side quest. That is the pack doing what it has always done: take the thing that already exists and make it bigger by hand.
