A Texas justice of the peace looked at the testimony, the photos, and the body condition scores and ordered the animals out. Most of them are already gone.

We Care Wildlife Sanctuary in Lakehills, Bandera County, lost the civil hearing. Justice of the Peace Gina Grothues issued the order September 15 after a two-day hearing. The sanctuary had ten days to appeal. It did not. By mid-October the county attorney confirmed 137 animals had been relocated to three 501(c)(3) nonprofits across Texas.

That is the concrete thing that happened. Everything else is what it costs when a sanctuary falls behind on the basics and the court steps in.

## What the hearing showed

The warrant came first, September 4. Bandera County Constable Greg Spengler, assisted by the sheriff's office, the American Humane Society, San Antonio Zoo veterinary staff, and volunteer livestock people, executed it at roughly 7 a.m. They found more than 20 dead animals, including approximately 15 horses and two primates in varying stages of decomposition. More than 100 living animals were seized that day for evaluation and treatment.

The civil hearing followed ten days later, as Texas law requires after a seizure. Grothues was clear on the limits of her power: this was not criminal. It was only about what happens to the animals.

Witnesses from the American Humane Society described the same pattern in pen after pen. Inadequate food and water. Unsafe enclosures with fallen fencing and sharp metal. Animal remains where living animals were housed. Cesar Perea, associate vice president of the rescue division, testified that animals were not receiving basic husbandry, food, water, or veterinary care. David Fuentes described skeletal remains inside nearly every pen that still held live animals. Photographs showed a dead foal being eaten by a dog, bone piles on tarps, carcasses in freezers, empty or brackish water troughs, and animals so thin they scored 4 or below on the 1-9 body condition scale. Those scoring 4 or below were seized immediately.

County Attorney Janna Lindig argued the conditions met the definition of torture and cruel treatment under Texas law: animals unreasonably deprived of food, water, and care. The gallery reacted out loud. Representatives of the sanctuary were present the first day and asked questions. They were not present when the ruling came down the next afternoon.

## The order itself

Grothues ordered removal of the horses, pigs, cows, and other animals covered by the judgment, with narrow exceptions for dogs, goats, one donkey, frogs, and certain birds. Animals that remain must be spayed or neutered. No breeding program. No new animals for five years. Carcasses must be properly buried or removed. Bandera County law enforcement may supervise the property for ten years.

"We will not have a breeding program there," Grothues said. On the carcasses she was direct: it is a health and safety issue.

The sanctuary owner, Armando Mendez, had ten days to appeal. No appeal was filed. The order stood. By October 16 the county attorney confirmed the numbers: 107 animals seized on the original warrant, additional animals covered by the subsequent order, total 137 relocated. Roughly 30 remain under the exceptions. Two hogs were still in county care and expected to go to auction. One snake died after transfer; witnesses had already described it as extremely thin during the hearing.

The three receiving nonprofits are not named in the public reporting. The animals are distributed across Texas. That is the operational outcome: capacity shifted by court order because the original capacity could not be maintained to the standard the law requires.

## Why this sits with us

We run a sanctuary in rural Tennessee. The daily math is the same everywhere: feed, water, fencing, shade, medical, documentation, and the quiet work of not letting the dead accumulate where the living still are. Body condition scores are not abstract. They are the difference between an animal that can still be helped and one that has already been written off by the conditions around it.

What moved here is not a novel disease or a sudden flood. It is a sanctuary that reached a point where outside witnesses, photographs, and a civil court concluded the animals were better off elsewhere. The five-year ban on new intakes and the ten-year supervision are the structural consequences. Once the court is in the pen, the operator is no longer the sole decision-maker about who stays and who goes.

For anyone who builds both software and animal capacity, the lesson is unromantic. Documentation, body condition tracking, carcass protocols, and veterinary relationships are not overhead. They are the difference between a hearing that ends with the animals still on your property and one that ends with them on someone else's. The American Humane Society people were on the warrant and on the witness stand. That is not a relationship you want to meet for the first time under a search warrant.

The owner has said in other reporting that prior raids ended with animals returned and that he views the timing as connected to other legal disputes. The civil record that mattered in September was the testimony and the photographs. The appeal window closed. The animals moved.

## What is left

About 30 animals remain under the narrow exceptions. The property is under the order's restrictions. The receiving nonprofits now hold the majority of the inventory that used to be under one roof. No criminal charges are described in the coverage that has reached the public. The civil outcome is already executed.

If you run sanctuary capacity, the number that matters is not the headline 137. It is the body condition scores that triggered the initial seizures, the remains found inside living pens, and the fact that the ten-day appeal window was allowed to expire. Those are the points where the story could have gone differently. They did not.

We will keep watching how the remaining animals and the receiving organizations handle the load. Capacity is finite. Court orders move it whether the original operator is ready or not.

The animals are not back. The order is in force. That is the status as of mid-October reporting that is still circulating.
