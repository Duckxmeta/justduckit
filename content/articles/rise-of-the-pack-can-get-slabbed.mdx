The boxes are hitting porches. The next move is a slab.

On October 6, @DDLTCG said Rise of the Pack preorders had all shipped and were being received. Rip the packs. Build the decks. Get ready for the first official championship in December at DDMIAMI. One legend will rule them all. The next afternoon the same account posted that Doginal Dogs Legends is officially fully live with AGS grading, and that holders can send cards in. The link went to the AGS live feed. @doginaldogs reposted it. This morning the official account put DDMIAMI at 60 days.

That is the week. Not a floor chart. A mail slot, a grading desk, and a December room.

## The graphic is a promise, not a census

The October 7 post carried a fan of slabs. Labels read 2026 Legends, Rise of the Pack. Full-art Gigatron sits in the middle at a GEM-MT 10, cert number on the label, #V071 under the name. Wiggles, Shiboshi, and the rest of the fan show 9.7s and 9.8s. It looks like a product shot from a grading company that already knows the set.

Treat it like a product shot. We have not pulled a population report that proves those exact certs are sitting in a vault. The post does not claim a Gem Mint Gigatron already sold. It claims the lane is open. You can send the cards in. The feed is public. That is the fact. The render is the invitation.

That distinction matters. A first-edition set gets ruined when people treat marketing slabs as completed sales. It also gets ignored when people treat a real submission path as a meme. This one is a submission path. The official account said fully live. The grading company linked is Automated Grading Systems — AGS, the RoboGrading shop — not a pack-made sticker.

## What AGS actually is

AGS grades with machine vision and 3D laser scanning, not a grader having a long Tuesday. Their own site says cards come back in about 20 days on the standard lane, that the front of a card weighs 60 percent of the grade and the back 40, and that a label can carry subgrades for centering, surface, edges, and corners. Pricing on the site is flat. The headline comparison they publish puts a standard submission near $18 and a 20-day window, with a value bulk tier at $15 a card if you send at least 30. Add-ons on the how-it-works page include an eight-subgrade report and a 3D laser map. They say they grade all major TCGs and that a card missing from the catalog can be added by hand.

Those numbers are AGS talking about AGS. We are not auditing their volume chart or their “ranked number three in TCG” line. What we can use is the shape of the offer: a third-party slab, a public feed, a turnaround measured in weeks instead of a season, and a price that does not require a PSA economy lottery ticket to even try.

For a set that left a Chelsea hotel in September and is still arriving in mailboxes in October, that shape fits. The chase cards are hand-drawn. The print runs are young. Holders who want a condition receipt before Miami do not have six months to wait on a backlog.

## Why a slab shows up now

Most new card games stop at the rip. The pack did not. Preorders sold. DDNYC got the first live opens and the first tournament. Secondary listings showed up after the bags left the venue. Now the same cardboard has a grading counter.

A slab does three jobs a raw sleeve does not.

It freezes condition. A full-art that survived a suitcase, a kitchen table, and a group chat photo has a story. It does not have a number. Once it has a number, the next trade does not start with “trust me, it’s clean.”

It creates a population. AGS runs a public feed and a pop report. If Legends cards start landing there, the set gets a condition census that is not a Discord screenshot. Early pops are the whole game on a first edition. A 10 that exists when almost nothing has been submitted is a different object from a 10 that exists after ten thousand submissions. We do not have that census yet. The door just opened.

It separates the play copy from the keep copy. Rise of the Pack is a game. Five classes. Forty-card decks. A December championship. You do not slab the deck you plan to shuffle in Miami. You slab the hit you are not willing to corner-ding on a hotel table. The rest stays in a sleeve, because a card that cannot be played is merchandise, and this set is trying to be both.

That split is the lesson. Inscriptions proved ownership. The physical set proved the culture could leave the chain. Grading proves the object can sit next to every other TCG on a shelf without a translator.

## Do not grade the pile

Fully live does not mean every common goes in a box to Staten Island.

Grade the cards you would be angry to scuff. Full arts. The chase pulls people are already photographing on the timeline. Anything you would rather show than shuffle. Leave the playset alone. A deck that cannot be unsleeved for the December room is a deck that lost the plot.

Check the catalog before you ship. AGS says missing cards can be added manually, and new-card research can stretch the turnaround. A brand-new set is exactly the case where that note matters. Confirm Rise of the Pack is selectable, or budget the manual add, before you treat the 20-day line as a promise.

Insurance tiers on their pricing page top out in the hundreds on the bulk lanes, not in chase-card fantasy numbers. If a pull is the one you cannot replace, read the declared-value rules before it leaves the house. We are not publishing a price guide. We are saying the slab is a tool, not a ritual.

And do not confuse the promo certs in the graphic with inventory. Until a real label shows up on the feed with a real scan, the GEM-MT 10 Gigatron is art direction. Good art direction. Not a comp.

## Sixty days is the clock

DDMIAMI is the room this cardboard is walking toward. Official post this morning: 60 days, best event yet, tickets and details on the events page. The championship call from October 6 sits on top of that. Preorders in hand. Decks to build. One legend.

Grading is the side quest that makes the main quest sharper. People who could not get a box in the first wave can still meet a slabbed hit before December and understand what the table is playing. People who did get a box can lock the copy they will not risk and sleeve the copy they will. Both moves make the set look like a product with a second life, which is the only kind of product that survives the flight home.

We do not need a sanctuary story to see it. We need a porch, a submission form, and a date in Miami.

The packs left the building. The mail is catching up. The slab lane is open.

Proof the pull. Sleeve the deck. Then say you are going.
