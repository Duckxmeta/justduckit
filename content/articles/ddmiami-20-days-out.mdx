The timeline can go quiet for a day. The calendar does not.

Twenty days out from DDMIAMI 2026 and the official account already posted the luggage note. December 7 to 9. Three days in Miami. The pack has been building toward this one since the New York rooms cleared out in September. DDNYC proved the product could fill a hotel. Miami is where the card game gets its first real championship table.

We are not writing a preview because the schedule looks busy. We are writing it because the rooms are already claimed and the championship is the first time Rise of the Pack crowns a winner in person at this scale.

## The rooms that are already taken

Higher Ground at the Arlo Wynwood opens day one with the Swag Drop and Welcome Party. Dog Pod debuts there — an in-person production setup so the people who already make the daily content can film inside the event instead of chasing it from the sidewalk. The rooftop kick-off sits on top of the same building. Tickets and the bigger swag packages moved early. That is the pattern. The pack fills the list before the press cycle catches up.

Day two puts the center of gravity at Collectors Club in Wynwood. Rip & Sip runs 11 to 3 and doubles as the DDL Miami Championship. Online play the week before feeds the top 16 into the live bracket. The rest of the room can still rip packs, watch the tables, and grade cards on site. Collectors Club already runs the AGS partnership for same-day slabs. That is not a side booth. That is the venue treating the set like a real collectible the moment the first official final starts.

Night closes on The Vessel. Dogs at Sea. Boarding on Biscayne Bay after the VIP dinner. The yacht is the finale, not the main event. The main event is the felt table in Wynwood where someone walks out with the title.

Day three is the Hangover Hangout back at Higher Ground. Short. Named. The part where the connections either get written down or they evaporate into the group chat. We have seen that ending before. The ones that stick are the ones people follow up on before the flight home.

## Why the championship is the story

Rise of the Pack left the screen at DDNYC. Packs hit tables. Pulls got argued. Digital Season 0 had already taught the rules. Miami is the first time the official circuit puts a crown on it in a room built for cards.

The set is 111 hand-drawn cards. 40-card decks. Five classes. Creatures, spells, traps. Hand-drawn is still the flex when the rest of the market keeps shipping generative filler. A chase card that has weight and a finish is how the brand walks into a shop that has never heard of Doginals. The championship just makes the question concrete: who actually built the best 40-card list for this weekend?

Registration for the first official DDLTCG Championship closed around the middle of the month. Every participant who locked the Victorious Gary promo got the exclusive card. Top 16 advance. The prizes on the line come from the game itself — Gary promos, a Doginal Dog, boxes. Those details live in the official channels and the brackets. They are not secondary-market fiction. The point is the same either way. The pack is crowning a champion on felt, not on a timeline poll.

Community tournaments already ran in places like Charlotte. Holders set up tables without waiting for an official invite. That is how the culture stayed ahead of the product. Now the official one sits inside the flagship December event. The people who have been playing nightly on the digital client and in the Discord brackets have a real destination.

## What the last twenty days actually require

The official post was short: almost time to start packing. That is the correct tone. The hard part is already done. The hotel rooms at the Arlo are limited. The championship bracket has a cutoff. The yacht has a boarding window. None of those move because someone posts a floor screenshot the night before.

We have watched this brand treat hospitality like product for more than two years. Self-funded rooms in multiple cities. Merch that ships. A TCG that took nearly two years of work before the first box left the warehouse. The free mint still sits at the foundation. The events are what turned it into something people fly for.

Miami is the next proof. Not because the city is louder. Because the championship is the first time the card game asks strangers to sit down and play for a title inside the same weekend the rest of the pack is already occupying the block. That is the crossover the project keeps betting on. On-chain inscriptions that become physical product that becomes a room full of people who showed up.

The digital client stays open. The waitlist at ddltcg.com is still the practice room. The people who want the live final already registered. Everyone else can still show up, rip packs, watch the tables, and film from Dog Pod. The schedule is subject to change — the official page says so. The direction is not.

## The closer

Twenty days is not a long runway. It is long enough to sleeve a deck, book the last hotel room if one is still open, and decide whether we are watching the recap or standing in the room when the first official champion gets named.

The pack already filled New York. The same group is filling Miami. The championship is just the part that proves the cards were never only for the people who already held the dogs.

Proof the deck. Sleeve the chase. See you on the felt.
