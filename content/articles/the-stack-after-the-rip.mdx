The boxes are still showing up. The brackets are already running.

On September 30, Kush — tournament host, KennelDAO builder, pack account — dropped a thread that did not ask anyone to wait for the next venue. Four links. Holder tools. Player tools. Tournament rails. All of them marked in development. None of them framed as a teaser for a bull market.

That same stretch of timeline had a holder staging hard cases for a Legends delivery and asking if the order was enough, and other pack accounts calling a DDLTCG night live. The cardboard is moving into rooms. The software is already sitting next to the table.

That is the story. Not another recap of Chelsea. Not another countdown to Miami. The pack is building the book, the binder, the forty, and the bracket while the first set is still being sleeved.

## Four doors, one afternoon

The thread is concrete. Kush and the KennelDAO team said they had been collaborating on the tools for months, through the quiet, and then listed them in order.

First is a Doginal Dogs evaluator at kushmedia.xyz/dd-evaluator. His description: an algorithm that prices a dog from traits and the trend of those traits against historical listings and sales. Floors and recent comps, with a refresh he put around twelve hours. A per-trait blend of listing floor and sale depth so one vanity ask cannot set the book. A compound base from the strongest rare layers. Bonuses only for scarce traits that are actually trading above that base. Rarity bands, demand multipliers, optional lifts for 1/1s, harmony, combos, and TCG-set ties. It never prints below the collection floor. Listed dogs get tagged undervalued, fair, or overpriced against the model. He said KennelDAO uses the same engine when they talk treasury value, and he labeled it not financial advice.

Second, on the same page, is the DDL TCG tab. Rise of the Pack values, a checklist, and a camera scanner. Tick the prints you own, or scan them. It tracks base commons and rares, holos, full arts, and a master set, and it names what is complete. Portfolio value sits on the checklist. Discord login so the profile travels. Export and import. A market panel with box MSRP, pack math, an eBay feed, and top-print estimates. He was plain about the limit: live eBay comps are still thin, so a lot of the book is pack math until more sales land.

Third is the deck companion at ddltcg.kushmetax.com. He called it unfinished and still usable. Look up a card. Learn class matchups. Build a forty with answers. Search by name, rules text, or serial. Filter by class, type, and trigger. Ready-made forties: five one-class lists and ten two-class mixes, ranked by a strength score that weights trap density, answers, engines, and finishers. Ambush, Frost Lock, and Typhoon show up in the weights because live players hold them. Paste a list, pick one, or generate your own. Save it. Copy the list. Copy the serials. Collection lives on the evaluator. Play lives here.

Fourth is legendsbrackets.com. His words: the official tournament rail for Doginal Dogs Legends nights, including a semi-IRL table layer. Discord handle you already use. No second account. Paid nights put the receipt on the roster. Paste the forty from the companion when you register. Hosts can lock class mix before doors. Single elim, double, Swiss, round robin. Live trees. Public standings. Records. ELO, credited to DickTheDev. King of the Hill on top of the tree. Watch from the rail with no account — brackets, standings, and table cams are public. Hosts keep their own rooms, confirm payments, run the tree, and kick an overlay when the stream is hot. They cannot touch anyone else's bracket or the ledger. Semi-IRL means lock a series, run the tree from the same board, voice in the official Doginal Dogs Discord, and leave a record when the last match ends. He said official DDL nights already run there.

We are not restating that last line as a press release. We are reporting what the host posted, and what the timeline did next.

## The night did not wait for the tool to freeze

Hours after the thread, pack accounts were still talking about that night's tournament. Order called it lit before it started. Metal posted the live link anyway. That is the useful part. The bracket rail is not a slide in a deck. People were already pointing at a room.

On the physical side, Boo posted a photo of the setup and asked the only question that matters once boxes leave the warehouse: am I prepared for the delivery, or do I need more hard cases. That is not hype. That is a kitchen counter turning into storage.

Put those two pictures next to each other. One person casing boxes. One night running on a public tree. The first set sold out its preorder, debuted in New York, and started circulating. The next problem is not awareness. It is operations. Binders. Deck lists. A way to sit down without building a production stack from scratch.

Kush closed the thread the same way a builder should. None of it is frozen. Evaluator most mature. Collector live, comps and scanner still tightening. Companion live as a deck builder. Brackets already running real nights while IRL and host billing get tighter. The brief, in his words: stop waiting for a bull to justify infrastructure.

## What a real game needs after the rip

A sold-out box is a moment. A game that keeps getting played needs boring objects around it.

A price book that refuses to treat one loud ask as the market. A checklist that knows the difference between a base rare and a holo, and that admits the comps are thin. A forty you can paste into a seat instead of rebuilding from a screenshot. A bracket a stranger can watch without an account, and a host who cannot rewrite someone else's ledger.

Most collectible drops stop at the unboxing video. The secondary market is the receipt that strangers will pay. We already wrote that. This is the next receipt: people inside the pack are writing the tools the table needs, in public, while the product is still arriving at doors.

That is community-owned culture in practice. Not a foundation blog post. A scanner, a serial search, a class lock before doors. Handmade cards on one side. Handmade software on the other. Both of them still rough in the places the builder named.

The Doginal side of the stack matters for the same reason. The dogs are the identity layer. The cards are the object you can hand to someone who does not care about inscriptions. If the evaluator and the TCG tab stay on the same page, a holder can move from a trait read to a binder without switching religions. KennelDAO talking treasury value off the same engine is the other half — the dogs are not only a picture. They are inventory someone is trying to count without pretending a floor screenshot is a model.

None of this replaces the official game. ddltcg.com is still the front door for the set, the art, and play. These tools are the side rooms pack members built because the nights were already happening. Use them. Break them. That was the ask at the end of the thread.

## What we do with it

Open the four links before you argue about them. If you hold dogs, run one inscription through the evaluator and read the breakdown instead of the headline number. If you have cards on a table, scan or tick the binder and see what the checklist calls complete — and remember the book is still mostly pack math. If you play, paste a forty into the companion and then into a bracket instead of keeping the list in a notes app. If you want to host, the rail is already public. Reach out to the people running it.

Do not treat unfinished as fake. He labeled the companion unfinished. He labeled the scanner as still tightening. He labeled the nights as already real. That is a better status report than a roadmap with a season attached.

The rip already happened. The delivery photos are up. The night went live. The stack after the rip is a checklist, a deck list, and a tree anyone can watch from the rail.

Proof the tools. Break what is wrong. Then sit down.
