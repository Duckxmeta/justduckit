## The whole game in one breath

Two players. Forty life. Forty-card decks. Five lanes. Ten mana max.

You win by dropping the other Hero to 0 HP. That is it. You do not have to memorize a novel. You do not need a binder full of banned-list lore. You sit down, shuffle, and play.

A turn is four beats:
1. **Mana goes up**
2. **Draw**
3. **Play and attack**
4. **End turn**

Creatures hold the lanes and fight. Spells hit now. Traps sit quiet until they snap. If you have ever understood *“I put a guy down and I swing,”* you already understand the spine of Legends.

Classes give the flavor — **Wizard, Crown, Zombie, Bow, Pirate** — and you can mix them. Neutrals go in any deck. Three copies max of any card. That is the whole construction rulebook for a first deck. Pick what looks cool. Tighten it later.

## The easy on-ramp (even if you have never played a TCG)

Start here. Do not overthink it.

### 1. Play the digital tutorial first
Go to [ddltcg.com/play](https://ddltcg.com/play). Hit **Teach / How to Play**. Fight a bot. Learn the lanes with zero social pressure. Same game, same 40/40/5-lane frame. This is the fastest way to feel the rhythm before you sit across from a person.

### 2. Learn the win condition out loud
*“I am trying to take their Hero from 40 to 0.”* Say it. Everything else is how you get there.

### 3. Sit at a table with one other person
You need two decks, something to track life (phone notes work), and something for mana 1–10 (dice, coins, a scratch pad). Second player gets the Coin. Opening hand is four cards. If a card in that first hand costs 5 or more, send it back, shuffle, draw a replacement. The game even helps you not brick on turn one.

### 4. Play messy on purpose
First games are supposed to be loud and ugly. Read the card. Pay the cost. Put the body in a lane. Swing when it is ready. Ask *“does this work?”* out loud. That is how every great table starts.

### 5. Then join the Pack
Follow [@DDLTCG](https://x.com/DDLTCG) and the community. People are already teaching, listing playsets, ripping packs with their kids, and talking tournament energy. You do not have to “be a TCG person” first. You show up, you play, you belong.

That is the whole funnel: **tutorial → one kitchen-table game → community**. No seed phrase required to feel the hype.
