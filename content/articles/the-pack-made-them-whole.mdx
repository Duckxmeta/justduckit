The marketplace paused. The dogs did not.

On October 26, six Dogecoin transactions spent 264 inscriptions that holders had listed on market.doginaldogs.com. The signatures used 0x82. The marketplace went quiet the next day with a notice that trading was temporarily paused. Independent trackers logged the on-chain facts: one recipient address, 0.338582 DOGE total out, 201 inscriptions arriving at that wallet and 63 associated with miner paths.

What followed was not a statement. It was a ticket queue.

## The queue that actually moved

Affected holders were told to open a ticket in the Discord. The process was personal. The team reached out one by one. They matched traits where they could. Where they could not, they offered a fair swap. Some replacements came back upgraded. Photos of the new dogs started appearing on X within days. Holders posted the before-and-afters themselves.

One holder described receiving a personal message, then a replacement drawn from a leader’s own collection. Another noted that the team was covering the losses out of pocket so no one was charged. The official account amplified the notes. Community accounts thanked Bark, Shield, and Shibo by name. The phrase that traveled was simple: trust the process.

Reporting that followed put an estimated figure on the personal coverage in the $500,000+ range. The exact number is not the point. The action is. The dogs came from existing holdings the team already controlled. No new mint. No charge to the affected wallets. A support thread, a match or a swap, and the piece back in the wallet.

## What the rooms actually showed

This is the same pack that filled Dream Downtown in September. The same pack that has been ripping Rise of the Pack boxes, grading cards through AGS, and organizing local tournaments without waiting for an official calendar. The same pack that keeps the daily Spaces running and the IRL rooms selling out months ahead.

When the listings were swept, the response did not route through a press release first. It routed through the Discord that already existed for the community. The people who show up for the card tables and the hotel takeovers showed up for the ticket system. The replacements went out while the marketplace stayed paused. Holders kept posting the new dogs. The timeline did not go silent.

That sequence matters more than any floor screenshot. Most collections that lose inventory to an exploit either freeze, issue a vague promise, or disappear. This one opened a private channel, matched the pieces they could, and moved the rest from the people who already held the largest stacks. The community then documented the arrivals in public. The receipt is the posts themselves.

## The product keeps shipping anyway

Legends did not pause. Community pack rips continued. The first local tournament in Charlotte had already happened. Miami tickets and swag for December had already sold out. Gigatron was live. The daily radio and Spaces stayed on schedule. The exploit hit one surface — the marketplace listings — and the rest of the surface stayed open.

That is the free-mint thesis under pressure. The collection started with no capital raise and no mint revenue. The founder covered the gas. The dogs were given to the community. When a technical surface failed, the same people who built the events, the plush, and the hand-drawn cards covered the loss from what they already held. The secondary market for the cards and the on-chain dogs continued to move around the paused listings.

Holders who opened tickets received contact. Some received upgrades. The marketplace remains paused while protections are added. The pack did not wait for the site to come back online to decide what the community was for.

## Why the personal part is the product

Automated replies do not reassure anyone who just watched a listed dog leave the wallet. A private ticket that turns into a real message from the people who run the brand does. Multiple holders described the outreach as the part that changed the wait from tense to manageable. The dogs that arrived were not generic replacements. They were matched or improved from collections the team already owned.

That is not a marketing line. It is the operational difference between a brand that treats holders as inventory and a pack that treats them as people who will be in the next room. The next room is already booked. DD Miami is in December. The championship is on the calendar. The cards are already on tables outside New York. The same Discord that processed the tickets is the one that organizes the play.

The exploit was real. The pause is real. The replacements are real because holders posted the new dogs and named the people who sent them. We do not need to invent a recovery percentage or a final dollar total. The direction is visible. The team used what they held to make holders whole. The community kept the rest of the product moving.

## What we do with it

If you were affected, the ticket path is still the documented route. If you were not, the same people who covered the loss are the ones shipping the next event and the next set. The marketplace will come back when the protections are in place. The pack does not pause while that happens.

The dogs came back from the people who already had them. The rooms stay open because the same people show up for both.

Proof the tickets. Keep the table set. See you in Miami.
