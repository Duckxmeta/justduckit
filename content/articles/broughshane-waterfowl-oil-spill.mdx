A volunteer walked down through Broughshane Waterfowl Park on a Thursday morning to feed the birds and smelled kerosene. The black swans were completely oiled. That was the start of a recovery that is still running more than a week later.

The park, run by the Broughshane Environment Wildfowl and Wildlife Trust in County Antrim, has been forced to close while crews pull oil out of the pond. Thirteen birds are dead. More than twenty others went to the USPCA for specialist care. The death toll kept climbing after the first rescue runs.

## What Moved

The Northern Ireland Environment Agency was alerted on Thursday 22 October. Inspectors confirmed home heating oil (kerosene) had reached the ponds and traced it to a nearby tank that had been vandalized. They put out absorbent booms and other countermeasures. Flooding the night before had already put enclosures under water, so the oil that entered through storm drains had nowhere to be blocked.

Chairman Gary Hayward called it a nightmare. The park holds 120 collection birds plus wild birds. Some of the ones lost were birds the team was close to. He told BBC News NI the volunteer who found them walked in, smelled the kerosene, and saw the black swans coated.

Local reporting later put the losses at two Trumpeter swans, the male black swan, and more than ten ducks. North Antrim MLA Paul Frew described the source as a deliberate heating-oil theft elsewhere in the village. Excess oil ran into the drains and straight into the park. He has said the site could stay closed five to six weeks while it is cleansed.

At the USPCA, wildlife supervisor Siobhan Hawthorn said her team has been bathing geese, ducks, and swans and dosing them with charcoal for the toxins they ingested. Not every bird arrived in time. Birds hide illness well; by the time you see it, the damage is often already done. Most of the collection birds are not native species, so they cannot go back until the pond is fully cleared.

Police have asked for information. The Trust has been taking donations and messages of support while the clean-up continues.

## Why This Hits a Sanctuary Runner

We run animals on ground that other people can reach. A pond looks contained until the drainage system upstream of it is not. One tank, one act of vandalism or theft, one night of rain, and the water that is supposed to be the safe part of the habitat becomes the delivery system for the poison.

Collection birds are not wild birds. They do not get to leave. The black swans and Trumpeters at Broughshane could not fly the problem off. The same is true for a lot of the domestic waterfowl that end up in sanctuaries or public parks. When the water goes bad, the only options are the ones humans can carry out: catch, wash, treat, house somewhere else, and wait for the source to be fixed.

The volunteer load is the part that does not show up in the first news hit. People who already do the morning feeds are now doing emergency washes, coordinating with vets who work free, buying towels from the local shop, and keeping the rest of the flock going while the pond is offline. That work does not stop when the cameras leave.

## What the Clean-up Actually Requires

Oil on waterfowl is not a surface problem. Feathers that are supposed to shed water hold the oil instead. The birds ingest it while they try to preen. Internal damage follows the external coating. Charcoal doses and repeated baths are the immediate tools. The longer the oil sits on the pond, the more birds can pick it up again.

NIEA says the waterway is improved but further work continues as long as it is needed. Hayward has said it will be weeks before the park can return to normal. Frew’s five-to-six-week estimate is the clearest public timeline so far. Until the oil is out and the enclosures are dry and safe, the collection birds that survived stay in temporary care.

The drainage path is the structural point. Flooding plus an open path from village storm drains meant the booms could not stop the oil. Any site that takes water from outside its fence has the same exposure. Vandalism or theft of a heating tank is not an edge case if the tank sits upslope of the habitat.

## The Numbers We Have

- Thirteen birds dead as of the BBC report on 1 November.
- More than twenty birds transported for specialist care.
- Two Trumpeter swans and the male black swan among the confirmed losses, plus more than ten ducks.
- Park closed, with estimates of five to six weeks of clean-up.
- 120 collection birds on site under normal conditions, plus wild birds that use the water.

These are the figures the Trust, the USPCA, NIEA, and local reporting have put on record. The exact final toll can still move while birds remain in treatment.

## Closer

A pond is infrastructure. When the infrastructure upstream of it fails, the animals inside it pay first. Broughshane’s volunteers are still in the middle of that payment. The tank is the source. The drains are the path. The birds are the ones that cannot leave.

If you run waterfowl, map the water that reaches your fence before you need the map. The morning feed should not start with the smell of kerosene.
