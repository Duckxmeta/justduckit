The marketplace paused. The dogs did not stay gone.

On October 26 a listing-signature vulnerability on the official Doginal Dogs marketplace let someone sweep inscriptions that holders had listed. On-chain records put the count at 264. Six transactions. One recipient address. Most of the dogs reached that wallet; some went to miners as fees. Trading stopped with a clear notice: temporary pause while protections got upgraded. AI-driven exploits were already moving fast across the industry that week. This one hit the pack.

What happened next is the part that does not show up in a typical exploit post-mortem.

Leadership opened a ticket system in Discord. They reached out to affected holders individually. Replacements came from personal collections held by the team—Christian Barker (Bark), Damien Galvin (Shield), and David Chaboki (Shibo). Trait-matched dogs. Fair swaps where a holder preferred something else. In some cases upgrades. The company later put the value covered above $500,000. Holders started posting the receipts in real time.

That is not a press-release sentence. That is dogs landing back in wallets while the rest of the timeline argued about floors.

## The room was Discord

Most of the work lived in a support queue, not a Spaces. Holders opened tickets. The team answered. One holder wrote that the team reached out personally and replaced a wizard from their own collection, matching traits or offering a clean swap. Another said dogs were being redistributed as the posts went up and told people still waiting to open a ticket. Photos of new dogs appeared. People who thought a specific piece was gone for good posted that one had landed.

The language stayed plain. Trust the process. Woof woof. Proud to be here. The same accounts that normally post pulls and Spaces clips were posting gratitude for the people who answered the ticket.

Bark put the decision in one line that has already circulated: a true community is there for you when times get tough. We wanted to exemplify this as crypto goes through these trying times. The names attached to the replacements are the same three the pack already knows from the daily Spaces and the events. No new spokespeople. No outside fund. The bags that had been building alongside everyone else covered the gap.

## What the archive and the posts both show

An independent archive of the incident documents the on-chain facts: the six transactions, the 0x82 signature constant in the listing code, the pause notice on the marketplace site, the recipient address. It does not invent a narrative about intent. It lists what can be checked.

The holder posts fill the other half. They describe the ticket process, the personal messages, the trait matches, the dogs that arrived. Some noted the work was still moving on Sunday. Some thanked the team by handle. The pattern is consistent enough that it does not require us to invent a single quote or claim we sat in every ticket.

The marketplace itself stayed paused while the replacements moved. View and share still worked. Buying and selling did not. That is the conservative move when a listing mechanism has been used against holders. The product side—Legends cards shipping, Gigatron running, Miami on the calendar—did not stop. The on-chain collection side got a pause and a manual recovery.

## Why this is the opposite of the usual script

Most projects treat an exploit as a timeline problem. Post a statement. Point to a bug bounty or a new contract. Let the market reprice the risk. Holders who listed become the ones who eat it. The team posts a thread and moves on.

Here the opposite happened on the holder side. Leadership used personal holdings to put dogs back. The estimated number they attached is large enough that it is not a rounding error. The process they chose is slow on purpose: tickets, outreach, matching. It is the opposite of a one-click airdrop of something else. It is the same dogs, or close enough that the person who lost a specific wizard can recognize what came back.

That choice does not erase the vulnerability. The archive shows the signature type that allowed the sweep. The pause shows the team treated it as real. The replacements show what they decided to do about the holders who were listed at the time. Both facts can sit in the same sentence.

## The product keeps moving

While the tickets cleared, the rest of the pack did not freeze. Rise of the Pack boxes have been landing. Graded cards are already a topic ahead of Miami. Same-day grading is scheduled on site at Collectors Club during the championship. Spaces continue. Gigatron keeps its island open. The daily broadcast habit that has run for more than a thousand sessions did not take a week off.

The exploit response sits inside that same operating rhythm. The team that ships physical product, runs rooms, and builds AI tooling also answered the Discord queue with dogs from their own wallets. The community that shows up for the events and the Spaces showed up in the replies to say the dogs came back.

None of this requires us to claim we processed a ticket or watched a specific transfer. The public posts exist. The company statement exists. The on-chain count exists. The lesson is the same one the pack has been running for two years: the culture is the people who answer when the room gets hard.

## What we do with the receipt

If you are still waiting on a replacement, the path is the same one holders have been posting: open the ticket in the official Discord and wait for the outreach. If your dog already landed, the timeline has been full of people saying so. If you are watching from outside, the signal is simple. A free-mint collection that fills TAO venues also covers holders from personal bags when a listing mechanism fails.

The dogs that left on October 26 did not have to come back. The team decided they would. That decision is now the receipt.

Proof the tickets. Keep the table open. The next room is still Miami.
