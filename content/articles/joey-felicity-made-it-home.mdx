Saturday morning two brown pelicans left their habitat at the Pittsburgh Zoo and Aquarium. By evening both were back inside, checked, and not injured. The names are Joey and Felicity.

## What moved on the river

The zoo put out a short statement around midday: the birds were outside the enclosure, staff were watching, animal safety was the priority. A hotline went up so people could call in sightings without approaching. Crews worked the Allegheny between the Highland Park Bridge and the 62nd Street Bridge. Joey came out of the river near Sharpsburg around 4 p.m. Felicity followed less than an hour later. More than a dozen public calls helped. Pittsburgh River Rescue, Three Rivers Waterkeeper, the Army Corps of Engineers, and the National Aviary all had pieces of the day.

That is the part that actually happened. No drama beyond the water and the calls.

## Why these two birds stay in a habitat

Joey, Felicity, and a third bird named Pierre arrived at the zoo in July 2025 from the Seacoast Seabird Sanctuary in Indian Shores, Florida. They carried permanent wing injuries from fishing tackle. Florida wildlife officials judged them non-releasable. Brown pelicans use those wings for the plunge that feeds them; the damage means they cannot hunt the way a free bird does. The zoo houses them with Humboldt penguins. The injuries are the reason the enclosure exists for them.

A permanent wing injury is not a temporary rehab case. Once the flight and the dive are gone, the bird needs a place that supplies food and keeps predators and boats off the list of daily problems. That is sanctuary math, whether the walls are a zoo habitat or a farm pond with a net over it.

## The thin line between inside and the river

Escape stories travel because people picture the open water. The practical piece is smaller. A bird that cannot reliably fly still has enough power and enough will to leave if a gate, a fence, or a moment of distraction lines up. Staff then spend the day tracking two animals that look like they belong on a river but cannot stay there. Public tips worked because the instruction was clear: do not approach, call the number. That is the same rule we use when a dumped domestic duck shows up on a marina dock. Hands off until the person who can actually move the bird arrives.

The zoo noted both birds will get health checks before they go back with the penguins. No injuries reported from the day outside. That is the outcome you want when the alternative is a bird that cannot feed itself on the Allegheny.

## What the day costs

River Rescue, the Army Corps, the National Aviary, and a hotline are not free. Neither is the habitat that has to hold animals that cannot be released. The same arithmetic shows up at any place that takes permanent cases: the feed bill, the veterinary time, the enclosure that has to stay closed, and the volunteers who show up when something walks or swims out. Pittsburgh had a dozen people willing to call instead of wade in. That is the useful version of public attention.

Joey and Felicity are back. The injuries that put them in Pittsburgh are still there. The habitat has to stay closed, and the next gate has to hold. That is the work.
