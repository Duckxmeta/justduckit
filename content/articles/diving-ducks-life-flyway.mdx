On November 4–6, partners from 18 organizations in 14 countries met in Sofia to lock in the operational plan for Diving Ducks LIFE. The Bulgarian Society for the Protection of Birds is the coordinating beneficiary. The project runs from September 2026 through August 2032 with a total eligible budget of roughly €12.6 million, of which the EU LIFE programme covers about €9.4 million.

The three species are the white-headed duck (*Oxyura leucocephala*), the ferruginous duck (*Aythya nyroca*), and the common pochard (*Aythya ferina*). All three are globally threatened. The white-headed duck is endangered; the other two are vulnerable. They spend the non-breeding season concentrated in a relatively small set of wetlands along the Black Sea–Mediterranean flyway. Those same wetlands also support dozens of other waterbird species, which is why the partners treat the three ducks as umbrella species: improve conditions for them and a wider suite of birds and the systems that hold them benefit.

## What the numbers actually cover

The project aims to restore or rehabilitate more than 20,600 hectares of priority wetlands and improve safety conditions across 217,000 hectares of breeding, staging, and wintering habitat. Specific threat-reduction targets include cutting bycatch in fishing gear by 50 percent, cutting poaching incidents by 50 percent, and cutting disturbance from stray dogs by 50 percent at key sites. Climate-smart restoration pilots are built in so the restored areas have a better chance of holding water under the conditions that are already shifting.

Those hectares are not abstract. In Croatia the partners include the public institution that manages Kopački Rit Nature Park and plan a substantial wetland restoration there. In Bulgaria the work includes action against illegal net fishing. Across the partnership the countries involved hold more than 90 percent of the white-headed duck’s Eastern Flyway wintering population, more than 60 percent of the EU’s ferruginous duck breeders, and major concentrations of common pochard. The list of partners runs from the Wildfowl and Wetlands Trust in the UK and Tour du Valat in France through organizations in Greece, Romania, Türkiye, Kazakhstan, Uzbekistan, and several Balkan states, with strategic cooperation extending to Azerbaijan.

## Why the scale matters for anyone running a sanctuary

A single sanctuary can perfect its own water management, predator exclusion, and veterinary protocol and still watch the birds it releases or the wild birds that use its ponds decline because the larger flyway is still losing habitat and still carrying the same mortality pressures. Diving ducks respond relatively quickly when habitat quality improves; that is one reason the project expects measurable population response rather than only process metrics. The same logic applies at any scale: the local work is necessary and never sufficient by itself.

The project is also an explicit response to the International Species Action Plans for these three ducks. The main anthropogenic threats listed in those plans—habitat degradation, illegal killing, bycatch, lead poisoning, and disturbance—are the same pressures that show up in smaller form on most working properties that hold water. Lead shot is still in the sediment of many older wetlands. Abandoned fishing nets continue to kill after the season ends. Stray dogs and unmanaged human access push birds off the water they need. The difference is that this effort is trying to address those pressures at the scale the birds actually use.

## Coordination is the hard part

Eighteen organizations in fourteen countries means eighteen different legal frameworks, funding calendars, and local politics. The Sofia meeting was the first full partner gathering after the formal start. The LIFE database lists the coordinating beneficiary, the full partnership roster, the Natura 2000 sites already identified in the EU member states, and the budget split. What it does not yet show is the week-to-week operational reality of getting a net pulled from a lake in one country while a restoration contractor is mobilizing in another. That gap is normal; it is also where most large conservation projects either produce lasting change or produce reports.

For a builder who also runs a sanctuary, the useful signal is not the press release language. It is the combination of a multi-year funding commitment, explicit hectare and percentage targets, and a partnership that already includes both the scientific institutions and the site managers. Those are the conditions under which wetland restoration has a reasonable chance of still being functional when the project ends. They are also the conditions under which smaller operators can point to a larger system that is at least trying to hold the flyway together.

The white-headed duck’s wintering numbers are concentrated enough that protecting a relatively modest number of sites moves the needle for the whole Eastern population. The same is true, at different densities, for the other two species. That concentration is both the opportunity and the risk: the birds are not evenly distributed, so the places that still hold them carry outsized importance. The project is an attempt to treat those places as a connected system rather than as separate national problems.

## What is still open

The formal start date is September 2026 and the first full partner meeting was early November. Site-level work plans and the exact baseline numbers are still being locked in. The LIFE database and the coordinating beneficiary’s statements give the targets, the budget, and the partnership roster; they do not yet give operational results. That is normal at this stage and is the point where the hectare and percentage commitments either survive implementation or do not.

Anyone whose daily work already involves clean water, predator pressure, and keeping birds fed is already working inside the same system these organizations just received multi-year funding to address at flyway scale. The monitoring over the next six years will show whether the reduction materializes. The existence of the funding and the explicit targets is the concrete development that moved this week.
