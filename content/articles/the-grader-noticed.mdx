The boxes started landing. Then the grading feed opened.

On October 7, @DDLTCG posted that Doginal Dogs Legends is officially fully live with AGS grading. You can send cards in. The link points to the AGS feed. Bark confirmed it the same day. Within hours people were already talking about submitting the whole 111-card set for 10s and joking about grading the shipping box itself.

That is not a rumor. That is a public product decision. Rise of the Pack left the preorder, left DDNYC, left the eBay search results, and is now sitting in the same pipeline that professional collectors use for sports and TCG product.

## The room that opened

AGS is not a side experiment. Alex Aleksandrovski, founder and CEO, runs it alongside Wooter Apparel and other collectibles work. On October 10 he posted a short video of Rise of the Pack cards — Mama Light, a holographic Bark — being looked at under lights. The caption was direct: “This game is something that caught my attention. The artwork and the playability is amazing!”

The post moved. Hundreds of likes, triple-digit reposts, replies stacking up. Pack members jumped in with their own clips. Outside the Discord, someone who grades cards for a living looked at the hand-drawn set and said the art and the game both hold up.

We do not need to inflate that. A grader noticing is not the same as a PSA partnership announcement. It is still the clearest outside signal we have had that the physical object is doing what it was built to do: survive contact with people who already collect cards for a living.

## What grading actually means here

Grading does not change the rules of the game. Forty life, five lanes, forty-card decks, five classes — Wizard, Crown, Zombie, Bow, Pirate — none of that moves because a card gets a number. What changes is the secondary layer. A graded card can sit in a case next to other graded product. It can be photographed for a listing with a standard the buyer already trusts. It can travel further without the seller having to explain “yes, these are real cards from a real game.”

The community has already been doing the hard part. Packs are being ripped on kitchen tables. Boxes are showing up in mail. Playsets are being assembled. The digital beta is live at ddltcg.com/play for people who want to learn the lanes before they sleeve anything. Grading is the next door, not the first one.

People started submitting almost immediately. One holder posted 13 cards received for grading. Others talked about chasing the first full set of 10s. That is normal TCG behavior once a set has enough volume and enough outside eyes. We are watching it happen in real time on a set that started as free-mint pixel dogs on Dogecoin.

## Handmade versus the feed

Most NFT-to-physical attempts die at the first photo. The art looks generated. The stock looks thin. The game, if there is one, has no rules anyone outside the Discord will sit down to learn.

Rise of the Pack is hand-drawn. One hundred eleven cards. The same team that has been shipping events and merch for years put the time in before the preorder opened. That is why a grading company founder can hold the cards under lights and talk about playability instead of just the brand name. The object has to work before the label does.

We are not claiming every card is a masterpiece or that every pull will grade high. We are saying the set was built so that question can be asked by people who grade for a living. That is a different standard than another Discord announcement.

## The calendar is already full

While the grading feed opened, the rest of the year kept moving. Preorders have been shipping. Booster packs and boxes are available on the official shop. The first official DDLTCG Championship is set for December at DDMIAMI. One Legend will rule them all. Tickets and event packages have been circulating for weeks.

Grading does not pause any of that. It sits next to it. Someone can rip a box tonight, play a few games, send the chase cards for grading, and still be building toward Miami. The physical product, the digital tutorial, the secondary market, and the event calendar are running in parallel because the same people care about all of them.

That is the free-mint-to-IRL loop working. The inscriptions gave the culture a permanent home on Dogecoin. Legends gave that culture something you can hold, trade, grade, and sit down to play without opening a wallet. The grader noticing is one more receipt that the second half of the sentence is real.

## What we do with it

If you have product, look at the AGS feed and decide what is worth sending. If you are still learning the game, open the digital tutorial and run a bot match. If you are hunting cards, the secondary is active and the shop still has packs. If you are aiming at Miami, the championship is the next room on the calendar.

None of this requires inventing a floor price or claiming we watched every submission. The official post exists. The founder’s video exists. The shipping updates exist. The championship date exists. The only job is to notice that the cards are now in more rooms than the ones we already booked.

The grader noticed.

We keep shipping the game.

Proof the pulls. Send what is worth grading. See you in the next room.
