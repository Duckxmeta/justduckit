The box hits the porch and the bracket is already full.

That is the week. On October 6, Legends TCG posted that Rise of the Pack preorders had all shipped and were being received, and pointed the pack at the first official DDLTCG Championship in December at DDMiami. On October 7, the same account said Doginal Dogs Legends is officially fully live with AGS grading, and linked the submission feed. On October 8, Thursday Mashup ran. Free seat. Best of three. Kush posted the bracket close with @Hofers as champion.

Three beats. Mail. Slab. Table. None of them are a recap of Chelsea.

## The announcement was short on purpose

The grading post is four lines and a link. No price sheet. No population report. No promise that a 10 changes the December field.

"Doginal Dogs Legends is officially fully live with AGS grading. You can send your cards in to get them graded."

That is the whole official note. The feed is agscard.com. AGS describes itself as an automated grading shop for modern sports and major TCGs, with services that start at $15 and turnarounds from same-day to 20 days on its own how-it-works page. We are not treating that site copy as a Legends rate card. The official post did not publish a Doginal-specific price, a card checklist inside AGS, or a turnaround for this set. If the catalog does not list a Rise of the Pack name, the submission flow says cards can be added manually. Verify that before anyone boxes a chase.

What the post does settle is the category. A hand-drawn first set from a Dogecoin inscription community now has a public grading lane. Not a custom slab a holder made in a garage. A third-party feed the account told people to use.

That is a different object than a pack rip photo.

## Mailers and brackets in the same forty-eight hours

Preorders were the first physical wave after DDNYC. The October 6 note said they have shipped and are currently being received. The timeline matches the note. Holders posted fragile boxes landing. Zack spent the morning of October 9 ripping what was left in a box and asking the pack for best pulls. Gambit called the digital client the most fun TCG on the board, with full card access for deck building at ddltcg.com. The physical and the client are running at once.

Thursday Mashup did not wait for the mail. Kush posted the event the night of October 8: 7:30 p.m. EDT, free entry, single elimination, best of three, cap 32, sign-in on Discord, bracket at legendsbrackets.com. Later he posted the close. Semis: @anthemhayek vs @JagOBX, @Hofers vs @akmetax. Final: @JagOBX vs @Hofers. Champion: @Hofers.

We were not in that chair. We are not inventing the games. The public bracket note is enough. A free online seat filled and closed while physical product was still being sliced open on kitchen tables. That is how a game becomes a circuit instead of a drop.

The December championship is the room those seats are pointing at. DDMiami is December 7 through 9. The October 6 line was plain: rip the packs, build the decks, one Legend will rule them all. Grading does not replace that sentence. It sits beside it.

## What a slab is for, and what it is not

A slab is a receipt that a card left raw and came back measured. Centering, edges, surface, a number, a case. Sports cards taught the hobby that language. TCGs borrowed it when a chase was worth more sleeved than played.

Rise of the Pack is still a game. Forty-card decks. Classes. Creatures, spells, traps. The Thursday bracket used cards as tools. A slab takes a card out of that job. That is the trade. Grade the copy you do not need on Thursday. Keep the playset you plan to sleeve for Miami. The set has a digital client for the nights the physical copy is in transit. Use it.

The trap is treating the first week of grading like a price event. Nobody official posted a pop report. Nobody official posted a sold slab. Replies under the announcement asked for full 111-card lots in 10s and joked about grading the shipping box. That is pack humor, not a market. The September eBay wave already proved strangers will bid on raw Legends. A slab is the next sleeve, not a new story about what the card is worth. Worth gets settled when a graded copy actually sells, in public, with the number on the case.

Hand-drawn matters here. Generative sets can reprint the same file until the chase is a setting. Rise of the Pack was drawn. Print runs, holos, and first-edition stock are finite in a way a screenshot is not. Grading is how a finite physical object gets a shared language outside the Discord. A cousin who has never opened a Doginal can still read a slab.

## The pipeline is the point

Inscription on DOGE. Free mint. Then a hand-drawn card. Then a table in New York. Then a mailer. Then a bracket on a Thursday night. Then a submission feed.

That order is the brand. Community-owned culture does not stop at the JPEG. It keeps adding a layer a stranger can hold. The slab is a boring layer. Boring is the compliment. PSA did not make baseball interesting. Baseball was already being played. The case showed up after the card had a life off the diamond.

Legends is early in that life. DDNYC was the first tournament. Thursday Mashup is a weeknight seat with a 32-cap and a posted champion. Miami is the first official championship on the calendar. Grading opening in the same week as the mailers is the collector layer catching up to the player layer, not replacing it.

We do not need a thought piece about authentication technology. We need the pack to notice the sequence. The product survived the hotel. It survived the porch. It has a place to go if a hit is too clean to shuffle.

## What we do with the week

Rip what arrived. Post the pull. Build the deck you will actually pilot, on the client if the paper is still in a mailbox. If Thursday's format fit, the next free seat is the rep. If a hit is a display piece, read the AGS flow before it goes in an envelope — service, turnaround, whether the card has to be added by hand. Do not grade the only copy of a card the Miami list needs.

The official lines are short because the work is not a thread. Ship the boxes. Open the feed. Run the bracket. Point at December.

The slab is open. The table was already open.

Proof the pull. Then decide if it plays or if it leaves the sleeve.
