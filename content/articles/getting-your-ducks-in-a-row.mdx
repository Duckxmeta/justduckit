So, you’ve decided to take the plunge into the wonderful, messy, and rewarding world of duck keeping. Whether you’re looking for backyard companions, natural pest control, or a steady supply of rich eggs, bringing ducks into your life requires more than just a pond and some cracked corn.

This guide covers everything you need to know about building your first flock from the ground up.

### 1. The Numbers Game: How Many and What Sex?

Ducks are intensely social creatures. A solo duck is a stressed duck.

- **The Minimum**: Never fewer than two ducks. Three to five is the sweet spot for a stable starter flock.
- **Lifespan**: Domestic ducks typically live 8–12 years (sometimes up to 15 with good care).
- **Drake-to-Hen Ratio**: Ideal is **1 drake for every 4–6 hens**. A 1:1 or 1:2 ratio often leads to over-mating and injury to the hens. An all-male bachelor flock is also possible if no females are present.

### 2. Sexed vs. Unsexed (Straight Run)

- **Sexed**: Hatchery has identified the gender (usually 90–95% accurate).
- **Unsexed / Straight Run**: 50/50 chance. Cheaper, but you need a plan if you end up with too many drakes.

### 3. The Great Gender Reveal: Drakes vs. Hens

**The Auditory Test**  
The classic loud “QUACK” is almost always a hen. Drakes produce a soft, breathy, raspy sound instead.

**The Drake Feather**  
Most male Mallard-derived breeds develop one or two curled feathers at the top of the tail as they mature. Hens’ tails remain straight.

**Size and Plumage**  
Drakes are often larger with thicker necks. In colored breeds they may develop iridescent green heads.

### 4. How to Build Your Flock: Age Matters

- **Hatching Eggs**: Most rewarding but most difficult. Incubation is usually 28 days (35 for Muscovies).
- **Raising Ducklings**: High-maintenance. Need a brooder, heat, and non-medicated starter feed. Never let them swim unattended until they develop waterproofing.
- **Started / Juvenile Ducks**: Past the brooder stage (4–8 weeks). Easier for beginners and still young enough to bond.
- **Mature Ducks**: Skip the baby stage entirely. You know the sex and laying ability immediately.

### 5. Where to Find Your Ducks

- Commercial hatcheries (Metzer Farms, Meyer Hatchery, etc.)
- Farm supply stores during spring “Chick Days”
- Local farms, Facebook groups, and livestock swaps
- Sanctuaries and rescues (a great way to give a bird a second chance)

Building a flock is a journey of learning your birds’ unique personalities. Once you’ve settled on your numbers and sourced your birds, the real fun—and the real work—begins.
