Feeding ducks is an art form that goes far beyond tossing bread at a pond. To keep a flock healthy, productive, and structurally sound, you have to understand their specific metabolic needs. From the critical requirement for niacin to keeping their water clean, here’s how to properly fuel your ducks.

### 1. Nutritional Needs: Breed Matters

- **Layer Breeds** (Khaki Campbells, Runners, etc.) need higher calcium to support consistent egg production without depleting their bones.
- **Heavy Breeds** (Pekins, Rouens) grow extremely fast and are prone to leg issues if their diet isn’t balanced.
- **Niacin (Vitamin B3)**: This is the most important duck-specific rule. Ducks need significantly more niacin than chickens. Without it, ducklings can develop bowed legs and permanent joint problems. Brewer’s yeast is a common and effective supplement.

### 2. The Commercial Menu: Rations for Every Stage

- **Starter Ration** (first 2–3 weeks): High protein (18–20%) to support rapid early growth.
- **Grower Ration** (weeks 3–20): Protein reduced to 14–16% to prevent the bird from outgrowing its skeletal structure.
- **Layer / Breeder Ration**: Moderate protein with high calcium (3–4%). Only feed this to active laying hens — high calcium can harm drakes and young birds.
- **Mixed Grains / Scratch**: Good as a treat or for winter energy, but never as a complete diet.

### 3. Feed Forms and Textures

- **Mash**: Fine and common for ducklings, but messy. Dampening it helps reduce waste.
- **Crumbles**: Ideal middle ground for growing birds.
- **Pellets**: Most efficient for adult ducks — less waste and more balanced intake.
- **Sprouts**: Soaking grains unlocks extra nutrients and turns them into living greens.

### 4. Grit and Feeder Maintenance

Ducks need grit (small rocks or crushed granite) to grind food in their gizzard. Always offer it in a separate dish.

Because ducks have wet bills, feed can quickly turn into a damp crust that molds. Clean feeders weekly to avoid aflatoxin risk.

### 5. How Much Do Ducks Eat?

A mature duck typically eats 0.25 to 0.5 pounds of feed per day. They eat more in cold weather and less when quality forage is available.

### 6. Foraging: Nature’s Multivitamin

Foraging provides insects, slugs, snails, worms, and mineral-rich greens. It produces richer egg yolks and stronger immunity, while also offering excellent natural pest control with less plant damage than chickens.

### 7. Hydration: The Lifeblood of the Flock

- Ducks must be able to fully submerge their heads to clean their nares (nostrils) and eyes.
- Never offer dry feed without water nearby — they use water to help swallow and are at risk of choking without it.
- Duck water turns into “mud soup” quickly. Scrub and refill daily. In winter, use rubber bowls or heated bases to prevent freezing.

Managing duck nutrition becomes straightforward once you respect their unique biology. Keep it clean, keep water available, and never skimp on niacin.
