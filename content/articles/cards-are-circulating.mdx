DDNYC put cardboard on tables. The week after is when we find out if it stays there or goes back in a box under a bed.

Last 24 hours the answer has been loud on the timeline and on eBay: the cards are circulating.

Base sets of the 92 commons listing and selling. Holos starting at a dollar and drawing bids. Full arts with multi-day auctions. Family pack rips with kids pulling chase. People who bought boxes in Chelsea are already moving duplicates so someone else can build a deck.

That is not official market-making. That is the pack doing what the pack does — showing up and building the next layer themselves.

## The product left the event

We already wrote the launch story. Preorder sold through. Rise of the Pack hit Dream Downtown. Tournament tables. Foil under stage light. The [earlier piece](/articles/ddl-tcg-left-the-screen) covered the two-year build and the day-one energy.

This is the quieter chapter that decides whether a TCG becomes culture or inventory.

A first edition only matters if it moves after the cameras leave. Most drops die in the group chat. People post the unboxing, then the box sits. Secondary never forms because nobody wants to be the first seller or the first buyer outside the inner circle.

Here the pack skipped that hesitation. Within days of the event, listings appeared. Auctions ran. Prices discovered themselves in public. A full base set that started low closed higher. Holos that looked like spare change started attracting eyes. The conversation shifted from “did you pull” to “what are you asking.”

That shift is the real launch.

## Liquidity is a community product

Crypto loves to talk about liquidity like it is a protocol feature. For physical cards it is just people willing to post, ship, and pay.

Watch the pattern this week. Someone lists a stack of holos with starting bids that invite participation instead of protecting ego. Someone else runs a 24-hour base-set auction and asks the pack if they should run it again. Family videos of kids opening packs show up next to the serious collector talk. The same accounts that host Spaces are also moving cardboard.

No central market-maker required. No official secondary storefront yet. Just Dogs who already trust each other enough to complete a deal over a platform most of us used for sneakers and vintage.

That trust is the asset. The cards are the vehicle.

If the only people who ever owned Rise of the Pack were the ones who flew to New York, the set would stay a souvenir. When the same set starts showing up in auctions that anyone can bid on, the IP starts traveling without a plane ticket. That is how a free-mint pixel dog collection ends up on a kitchen table in a town that never heard of Doginals.

## What the secondary actually proves

Three things, none of them price targets.

First, the print run is finite and people treat it that way. First-edition language shows up in the listings for a reason. Collectors know the difference between a launch set and a reprint. The pack is already speaking that language without being told.

Second, the art carries. Hand-drawn cards do not need a crypto explanation to look like something worth sleeved. A base set selling in the open market means someone who never opened a wallet still found the pictures compelling enough to bid. That is the onboarding the team has talked about for months — the kind that does not require a seed phrase tutorial.

Third, the community will not wait for perfect infrastructure. Official digital beta, sanctioned play, shop wholesale forms — all of that is still in motion. Meanwhile the pack is already redistributing physical product through the tools that already exist. eBay is not glamorous. It is effective. And effectiveness is the brand’s actual style.

## The lesson is operational

Most Web3 projects outsource demand creation to influencers and then act surprised when secondary never appears. The Dogs did the opposite sequence: years of rooms and Spaces, a sold-out physical event, a product people could hold, and then the same people who showed up in Chelsea started the market work themselves.

That sequence is not accidental. It is the same muscle that filled Dream Downtown. Show up. Ship the thing. Keep the conversation going after the official program ends.

Secondary markets for TCGs are usually dominated by a handful of professional flippers. Early on, this one is still full of the same handles that host the daily Spaces. That will change as volume grows. The important part is that the first liquidity came from inside the pack instead of from outside capital trying to extract it.

## What we are taking home

1. **Circulation beats nostalgia.** A booster that stays in the attic is a memory. A card that changes hands is culture still moving.
2. **The pack can bootstrap its own market.** We did not need a polished secondary platform to start price discovery. We needed people willing to list and people willing to bid.
3. **Physical IP travels farther than the feed.** Every successful auction is one more address that now has Legends in the house. That is reach the timeline cannot fake.

Rise of the Pack left the hotel. The pack is making sure it does not stop there.

Proof the listings. Sleeve what you keep. Move what you do not. The next table is already being built by whoever is willing to ship.

Until next time. Run it back with the pack.
