On October 26 the official marketplace listing signatures got reused. Six Dogecoin transactions spent 264 inscriptions that holders had listed. The site went dark on trading. Holders opened Discord tickets. The leadership team started matching traits from their own bags and sending dogs back.

That is the sequence. Not a press cycle. A Discord queue, then replacements that looked like the ones that left.

## The room that mattered was the ticket thread

Most collections treat a listing exploit as a timeline problem. Someone posts a wallet. Someone else posts a floor. The project posts a pause notice and the conversation moves to the next candle.

Doginal Dogs opened tickets. Holders described the team reaching out one by one. One holder posted that the team contacted her personally about the wizard that left. Another said the replacement arrived upgraded. Photos of the new dogs started showing up in replies. The work was still moving on Sunday. One person was still waiting. The team kept sending.

Bark posted that hard times show who stands with you. The replacements came from the personal holdings of Bark, Shield, and Shibo. A company release later put the covered value above $500,000. Independent trackers counted 264 inputs spent with the same sighash type the marketplace client used for listings. 201 reached the recipient address. The rest went to miners.

The marketplace still shows the pause notice. View and share still work. Listings sit at zero while the upgraded protections roll out.

## What the pack actually did

They did not wait for an insurance product that does not exist on Dogecoin inscriptions. They did not issue a statement that the marketplace terms limited liability and then go quiet. They opened a support channel, matched what they could, and paid the difference out of the bags they already held.

That is not abstract community. That is concrete. A holder loses a dog they listed. A founder sends one that matches the traits or offers a fair swap. The holder posts the photo. Other holders see the pattern and open their own tickets. The queue moves.

We have watched collections disappear after smaller hits. The floor collapses, the Discord empties, the team starts talking about a pivot. This pack kept the calendar. Gigatron is running. Rise of the Pack cards are in hands. DD Miami is still on the board for December. The daily Spaces continue. The replacements went out while the market stayed paused.

## The lesson sits in the bags

Community-owned culture only works when the people who own the brand also own the downside. A free mint in 2024 did not create that. Events, plush, a TCG, and a virtual world did not create that by themselves. What created it was the decision to cover the loss from personal holdings instead of leaving holders to absorb it.

Most teams optimize for the upside. Floor screenshots. Volume tweets. The rare ones keep a reserve that can actually move when something breaks. Doginal Dogs treated the bags as working inventory for the pack, not as personal exit liquidity.

The independent archive at doginaldogs.info tracked the on-chain facts: the six transactions, the 0x82 signatures, the single recipient, the pause notice. Holders tracked the replacements in public posts. Both records exist. One is the exploit. The other is the response.

## Still shipping

The marketplace is not back. Trading stays paused while protections get upgraded. Holders who listed are still the ones who took the hit. The team covered what it covered from its own holdings. That does not erase the exploit. It does show the difference between a project that posts about community and a pack that opens tickets and sends dogs.

We keep watching the rooms that matter. Discord queues. Spaces. The Miami calendar. The cards that left the screen and hit tables. The pixel dogs that stayed with holders because someone decided the bag was for the pack.
