The packs left Chelsea. The cards did not stay in the hotel.

Two weeks after DDNYC, the loudest Doginal Dogs Legends conversation is not a recap thread or a floor screenshot. It is listings. Holos starting at ninety-nine cents. Full arts with real bids. Complete base sets pitched as starter piles. A Bark Meta signed card floating five-figure asks. Community accounts posting screenshots of auctions that actually move.

Official channels are not running this market. The pack is.

That is the story.

## What actually moved

Rise of the Pack hit the tables at Dream Downtown. Packs got ripped. A tournament ran. People walked out with cardboard that used to live only on a product page. Then the timeline did what timelines do with physical product that has chase: it started trading it.

Early listings showed up right after the event. By mid-September the secondary was loud enough that independent coverage called it heating up. Holos and ultra rares changing hands in the hundreds. Some higher. Playsets and full collections drawing more attention than random singles. One community member listed a triple base set — three of every base card, 276 cards total — and the replies were simple: someone is going to take it.

Bark posted the obvious: cards already moving at multiples of original cost, volume picking up, community eating. The official account did not need to seed it. The people who bought boxes at the event or through the preorder were already listing.

That is not a marketing plan. That is demand with a tracking number.

## Why secondary is the real test

A free-mint collection can fill a room. A sold-out preorder can feel like momentum. Neither tells us if the object has legs once the hotel lights go off.

Secondary does.

When holders start pricing the cardboard themselves, two things become clear. First, the product survived the event. Second, the culture is willing to treat it like a real collectible, not a souvenir. Playsets matter more than singles because people are building decks, not just flipping one shiny. Chase cards matter because someone is willing to sit on an auction for days and still get bids.

Crypto has a habit of celebrating the drop and ghosting the after. Rise of the Pack is still loud in the after. That is the difference between a merch moment and a brand that ships something people keep.

## Hand-drawn still carries weight

The set is 111 original hand-drawn cards. Five classes. Creatures, spells, traps. Built for 40-card decks. The art was never generative filler. That choice is doing work now. When a holo or full art lands on eBay, the buyer is not paying for a PDF. They are paying for stock, foil, and a piece of IP that started as Dogecoin inscriptions and ended up on felt.

Community accounts are not shy about it. Some are listing to move volume and spread the game. Others are testing price. Both behaviors are healthy. A market that only exists inside a Discord is not a market. A market that shows up on eBay with public bids is legible to people who have never heard of Doginals.

That is the onboarding the team has talked about since the announcement. Card shops. Kitchen tables. People who will never open a wallet extension. The secondary is the first public proof that the cardboard can travel without a Spaces host explaining it.

## What we are not doing

We are not inventing prices. Numbers move. Asks get ignored. Sold comps matter more than screenshots of starting bids. If a five-figure signed card sits, it sits. If a full art clears at a number that makes the room pay attention, that is data, not gospel.

We are not claiming the official team is driving the listings. They are not. The meta right now is holder-led. That is the feature. A project that has to manufacture secondary heat is already behind. A project where the people who showed up at the hotel start pricing the product themselves is doing the harder work.

We are not treating every auction as a signal to FOMO. Volume and completed sales tell a cleaner story than open asks.

## The pack already knows the next room

DDMiami tickets are live. Box of Awesomeness is back on the shop. The calendar did not stop at Chelsea. People who flipped cards or still have sealed product are already talking about using the secondary to fund the next flight. That is not financial advice. That is culture treating the physical product like it has a life after the event.

The same spine that showed up in the DDNYC recap still holds. Show up. Ship something you can hold. Let the room decide what it is worth once it leaves the venue.

Rise of the Pack left the screen at the hotel. It left the hotel on eBay. The timeline is still loud about the cards for a reason.

Proof the comps. Sleeve the ones we keep. And if the only place the game lived was a product page, this week is the reminder: the pack is already playing the next hand without waiting for permission.

Until next time. Run it back with the pack.
